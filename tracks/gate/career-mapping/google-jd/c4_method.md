# C4 method (2025 pilot): exact rules, thresholds, drops, limits
Scope: postings are only those Wayback archived. 2019-2022 postings are title-based only; descriptions cover postings archived from mid-2023 (capture years 2023-2026). This pilot: description-capture year 2025, n = 194 (security_engineering 71, threat_intel_and_response 67, sales_and_consulting 30, other_security 15 W, physical_corporate_security 11 W; W = n<30). Everything is OFFLINE (no network request in C4). One script regenerates every number: `c4_pilot_2025.py` (run with ../no-github-remote/web-scrap/.venv/bin/python3). Inputs: scraper-data shard `google-security-jd-2025` (via wayback_google_details._load_state); each record has the parsed sections about / minimum_qualifications / preferred_qualifications / responsibilities (all 194 have all four; 0 unknown headings) and the theme from the posting index. Outputs: c4_terms_2025.md (tables), c4_terms_2025.json (all 945 retained terms with counts by section and theme), c4_sample_2025.txt (postings read), c4_quote_check.txt, findings/2025.md.

## Term extraction (data-driven; no fixed skills list)
1. Text used: the three sections minimum (required), preferred, responsibilities. The 'about' section is NOT used (company boilerplate, salary text).
2. Degree lines are removed before extraction: any line matching bachelor / master / PhD / degree / doctorate. Reason: the degree line is in 190 of 194 required sections; the >90% rule would have removed it, but its variants ("related technical field", "practical experience") pollute the term list. Degree wording is analysed separately by regex (Table 'Stated years' / degree tables).
3. Lower-case, curly quotes normalised, possessive 's removed, hyphen between letters/digits turned into a space (cross-functional = cross functional), "cyber security" merged into "cybersecurity". Text split into clauses at newline , ; : ( ) [ ] ! ? . (sentence end), spaced dash, spaced slash, &. Tokens keep + # . / inside (c++, ci/cd). Single-letter tokens dropped except "c" (blind spot: the languages R and Go/C as single letters are partly lost).
4. Candidate terms = every 1-, 2- and 3-word sequence inside a clause that does not start or end with a stopword or number, has no inner stopword other than "of", and contains no company word (google, alphabet, mandiant). Stopwords = English function words plus filler words (experience, years, ability, strong, including, knowledge, understanding, work, working, related, relevant, preferred, minimum, qualification, equivalent, practical, skills, team ...; full list in the script, STOP).
5. Variant merging: crude stemming (plural s / ies / sses); the displayed label is the most frequent surface form (so "certifications", "vulnerabilities" appear plural). Verb forms are NOT merged (develop / developing / development stay separate). No synonym merging.
6. A posting counts once per term and section. Candidate threshold: the term is in >= 5 postings (any section). Boilerplate rule: drop terms in more than 90% of the 194 postings; after step 2 this dropped 0 further terms (the highest, 'security', is in 84%).
7. Subsumption: a shorter term is dropped if a longer retained phrase containing it covers >= 80% of its postings (179 terms dropped; e.g. a term that only occurs as part of a longer phrase). Retained: 945 terms.
8. Generic single words are dropped as UNIGRAMS only (phrases containing them stay): a hand list chosen after inspecting the first pass, whose top unigrams were technical, systems, field, management, develop, design, development, tools, languages, stakeholders, technologies, reviews, project, lead, practices, strategies, solutions, collaborate, drive (see GENERIC_UNI_RAW in the script for the list). This list is a judgement; it removes noise but also removes words that carry some meaning in isolation (management, design, analysis). Their phrases (project management, design reviews, threat analysis...) are kept and are the informative forms.
9. Reporting: for every retained term, postings containing it (n, % of the denominator) per section and overall, and per theme with the theme's n as denominator; cells with n<30 are flagged W. Top 40 overall (unigrams+phrases, and phrases only); top 30 per section; top 15 per section for each theme with n>=30 (security_engineering, threat_intel_and_response, sales_and_consulting); theme-distinctive terms (theme share minus share elsewhere in percentage points, term in >= 15% of the theme's postings and >= 5 postings); other_security and physical_corporate_security shown separately as weak/anecdotal (physical is not cybersecurity).
10. Also by regex (heuristic): highest "N years" in required text; Bachelor / Master / PhD wording; fields named in degree lines; median section length; duplicate-text count.

## Qualitative reading
15 postings chosen with random.Random(2025) inside theme quotas (4/4/3/2/2), read in full (c4_sample_2025.txt); notes in c4_qualitative_2025.md. All quotes (29 in the notes and the findings file) are machine-checked by the script to be verbatim substrings of the stored description text of the named job_id, <= 25 words, with the right theme (c4_quote_check.txt). [[term|section|theme]] placeholders in the hand-written narrative are replaced by counts computed from the data, so no number in findings/2025.md is typed by hand except the years/degree lines that cite Table 'Stated years' (checked against that table when written).

## Limits
- Snapshot: the text is what the page said at its capture date (2025), not the wording when the posting first appeared; 2019-2022 postings are title-based only (no recoverable descriptions), so nothing here says how these roles were worded before mid-2023.
- Phrase matching is a heuristic: exact words only, English only, no synonyms; a term absent from the list may still be asked for in other words; frequent generic words survive (e.g. 'time', 'conduct', 'program').
- Themes are assigned from titles earlier in the project; misassignments exist (qualitative note 2). Physical/corporate security must not be mixed with cybersecurity.
- Near-duplicates: 183 distinct (required+responsibilities) texts among 194 postings; repeated postings (e.g. one job in several cities) are counted per posting. Templates (the generic software-engineer text) inflate terms like design reviews or checking code in security_engineering.
- Only postings Wayback archived; no claim about all Google postings. n<30 cells are weak.
- One year only: no trend. Causes are inference and are not asserted.
- google-live/details-checklist.md was NOT read (optional comparison skipped to keep the pilot independent).
