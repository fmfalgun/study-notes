# C5 method (all years; supersedes the pilot's c4_method.md where they differ)
Scope: postings are only those Wayback archived. 2019-2022 postings are title-based only (no recoverable descriptions); descriptions cover postings archived from mid-2023 (capture years 2023-2026). Everything is OFFLINE (no network request in C4 or C5). One script does all years: `c4_years.py <2025|2024|2026|2023|drift|all>` (run with ../no-github-remote/web-scrap/.venv/bin/python3; resume = just re-run it; inputs = scraper-data shards `google-security-jd-<year>` read via wayback_google_details._load_state). The pilot script c4_pilot_2025.py and its outputs (c4_terms_2025.*, c4_narrative_2025.md, findings draft) are kept as the record of the pilot but use the OLD degree-line rule; c5_* files and findings/*.md come from c4_years.py.

## Rules that are identical for every year
1. Text: sections minimum (required), preferred, responsibilities; 'about' not used for terms. Empty parsed sections are treated as empty text (counted in the section-length table).
2. **Degree-line rule (fixed per entry [10]).** Before phrase extraction drop only lines that ARE the degree requirement: a line that starts (after any bullet) with bachelor / master / PhD / doctorate / degree / associate, or contains "bachelor('s) degree" or "master('s) degree". Lines that mention a degree only as an alternative ("... or 1 year of experience with an advanced degree") are kept. Effect on 2025 security_engineering required: python 23 (was 20), regex 23; c++ phrase 23, regex 25 (the phrase extraction misses "c/c++" written without spaces).
3. **Boilerplate rule** rechecked after the fix: terms in >90% of a year's postings are dropped. 2025: 0 dropped (highest 163/194 = 84%); 2024: 0 (84/100); 2023: 0 (19/26); **2026: 1 dropped, 'security' (83/89 = 93%)**.
4. Phrase extraction otherwise as c4_method.md steps 3-9 (lower-case, curly quotes normalised, possessive removed, hyphen between letters split, clause splitting, 1-3-grams with no leading/trailing stopword, company words removed, crude plural stemming, candidate threshold >=5 postings, subsumption at 80%, hand list of generic unigrams). Blind spots: words joined by a slash without spaces ("c/c++", "security/incident response") are one token; verb forms not merged; no synonyms.
5. Reporting: per term, postings in each section and any section; per theme with cell denominators; W = n<30. A year with n<30 in total (2023) gets counts only, no percentages.
6. **Headline numbers use documented regexes** (Table 'Regex metrics' in every c5_terms file lists each regex; applied to the raw section text, lower-cased, curly apostrophes normalised, same regex for every year). Master/PhD wording = `(?<![a-z])(?:master'?s?|ph\.?\s?d|doctorate|doctoral)(?![a-z])` in the preferred section (includes "masters" without apostrophe; 'advanced degree' is counted separately because it usually sits in "N years of experience with an advanced degree" equivalence lines). Every number in a findings file is tagged [regex] or [phrase-term]. Reconciliation table (in each c5_terms file and in findings) lists the postings where the two methods differ: 2025 clearance regex 27 vs phrase 'security clearance' 24 ("top secret clearance" without the word security); incident response required 23 vs 22 (one "security/incident response" with a slash); c++ 37 vs 35 ("c/c++").
7. Years of experience: highest "N years" figure in required (regex `(\d{1,2})\+?\s*(?:or more\s*)?years`); a lone "1 year" is not matched.
8. Requirement templates (regex, all years): software-engineer template = required has 'software development' AND ('programming language' OR 'data structures'); security-engineer template = required has 'security assessments' AND 'security protocols'. These are my operational definitions, not Google's.
9. **Qualitative reading:** seeded random sample inside theme quotas (2025: 15 postings, 2024: 13, 2026: 12, 2023: 8), each read in full (c5_sample_<year>.txt); notes in c5_qualitative_<year>.md. Every quote in the qualitative, narrative and drift-notes files is machine-checked by the script to be a verbatim substring of the stored text of the named job_id, <=25 words, with the right theme (c5_quote_check_<year>.txt; drift notes are checked against all years). Narrative numbers are placeholders resolved from the data, so no count is typed by hand except in c5_drift_notes.md, whose figures were copied from the generated drift tables.
10. **Drift (c5_drift.md).** Years compared: 2024, 2025, 2026 (2023 is weak and excluded). A group is compared only if n>=30 in every year: ALL and security_engineering. threat_intel_and_response (25, 67, 17), sales_and_consulting (15, 30, 14), other_security and physical_corporate_security are not compared. A term x section difference is listed if >=10 percentage points between two years AND the posting count behind the higher share is >=8 (my reading of '>=8 postings'); each row also shows the same change on distinct-text postings. Many combinations are tested (counts in the file), so listed rows are candidates; "stayed the same" = term in >=20% of postings in some year and highest minus lowest share <5 pp.

## Limits
- Snapshot at capture date, not at first appearance; each year = postings archived with a new-format page in that year (not a random sample of Google's roles; the theme mix differs by year).
- Phrase matching and regexes are heuristics; a term absent from a posting may be asked for in other words.
- Themes come from titles; questionable labels found in reading (2024: 3 of 13; 2025: 1 of 15; 2026: 2 of 12).
- 2023 (n=26) is weak: description only. 2019-2022: title-based only. Causes are inference and none is asserted.
